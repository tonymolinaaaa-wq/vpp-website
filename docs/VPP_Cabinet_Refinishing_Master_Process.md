# VPP Cabinet Refinishing — Master Process

**Source:** Ricardo's walkthrough, April 2026.
**Purpose:** Single source of truth for VPP's cabinet refinishing process. Feeds website copy, estimate descriptions, client communications, GC outreach, future SOPs, and training materials.

---

## PRE-ARRIVAL

- Customer empties all drawers and cabinets completely (dishes, belongings out of work environment, keeps items clean, allows prep and deep clean)

---

## PHASE 1 — DOCUMENTATION & LABELING

1. Take before pictures of kitchen cabinets
2. Label all doors and drawers — one piece of tape inside the base opening, one on the door/drawer face (temporary, for counting)
3. Number sequentially top to bottom, left to right — all doors and drawers get numbers, no distinction between door and drawer in the count
4. **Top doors:** permanent marker number written on the TOP hinge hole — this indicates the drill side for hanging hooks (holes will never be visible after reinstall)
5. **Bottom doors:** permanent marker number written on the BOTTOM hinge hole — same principle, drill side stays hidden

---

## PHASE 2 — DOOR & DRAWER REMOVAL

6. Remove doors one by one from hinges
7. Place face down on kitchen island with handles floating so door lays flat
8. Three empty clean 1-gallon buckets for separate storage: hinges, handles, screws
9. Lean removed doors against garage wall
10. **Drawers:** remove, numbered tape inside base opening and inside drawer, take to garage with doors

---

## PHASE 3 — MASKING THE KITCHEN

11. Mask floors with 1.5 mil plastic
12. Mask all walls surrounding kitchen cabinet bases
13. Set up EZ poles with 1.5 mil plastic curtain to enclose kitchen area ("the bubble")
14. Strategically place a zipper on best side for in/out access

---

## PHASE 4 — DEEP CLEAN CABINET TOPS

15. Before any sanding — deep clean tops of cabinets with degreaser (greasiest area in any kitchen, the area nobody looks at or wants to touch)
16. This happens after the bubble is set up but before sanding begins

---

## PHASE 5 — SAND & PREP BASES

17. Sand inside frames of openings FIRST — before masking openings (sanding after masking rips tape and doubles the work)
18. Clean sanded frames with damp rag (removes dust so tape adheres properly)
19. Mask all openings to keep primer and paint only on exterior surfaces
20. Sand exterior faces of cabinet bases with electric sanders (not by hand — we want proper scuffing for long-lasting adhesion)
21. Sand crown molding by hand (same principle as door detail grooves)
22. Remove all tools from kitchen bubble
23. Dust everything with duster brushes, vacuum all surfaces including countertops and floors (prevents sand dust from rising during priming)
24. Wipe down all cabinet bases with damp rags — crucial for primer adhesion

---

## PHASE 6 — PRIME KITCHEN BASES

25. Set up Festool filter to contain dust, primer, and paint fumes
26. Spray primer on kitchen cabinet bases
27. Let dry — takes longer indoors with AC running (bases continue drying while garage work begins)

---

## PHASE 7 — GARAGE SETUP (while bases dry)

28. Set up canopy on/near garage
29. Set up foldable table under canopy
30. Set up drying racks inline against a wall, enough width spacing to hang doors

---

## PHASE 8 — DOOR & DRAWER PREP

31. Stack all doors against wall with numbered hinge hole tape facing up — bottom doors flipped upside down, top doors right side up (all drill holes face same direction for batch drilling)
32. **If old hardware holes need patching:** patch BEFORE primer (primer seals the patch)
33. Drill 1/16" holes on indicated side of each door for screw-on hooks (do NOT install hooks yet)
34. Sand all doors — flat areas with electric sanders, detail grooves and edges BY HAND (do not skip any surface)
35. **Why we sand everything:** lost count of how many kitchens we've had to fix because other painters skipped sanding grooves and detail — paint peels off within months. Same principle applies to crown molding on bases.
36. Dust with compressor gun/nozzle — make sure no sand dust remains
37. Double check every door and drawer with damp rag wipe-down
38. Install screw hooks into drilled holes on all doors
39. **Drawers:** sand, dust, and wipe same as doors. Mask drawer boxes with masking paper or contractor film (professional grade plastic). Drawers don't get hooks — they sit stacked standing on opposite side, drawer face up.
40. Hang all hooked doors on drying racks

---

## PHASE 9 — SPRAY BOOTH SETUP

41. Set up pop-up spray booth (~4×4 ft)
42. Lay plastic on driveway under booth (prevents overspray on driveway)
43. Set up a rack inside spray booth identical to drying racks — doors hang vertically inside booth for spraying

---

## PHASE 10 — PRIME DOORS & DRAWERS (Assembly Line)

44. **Two-person system:** one person passes doors one by one to the sprayer → sprayer sprays the door → hands it back → other person hangs it on the garage drying rack while simultaneously grabbing the next unprimed door to hand to the sprayer
45. Repeat until all doors are primed
46. **Drawers:** two stacked buckets (open end down, taped at center) serve as a stand. Set drawer on stand, spray back side, flip, spray front side.
47. **Drying:** Arizona heat — primer dry to touch in 30–45 minutes outdoors

---

## PHASE 11 — POST-PRIMER DETAIL WORK

48. Once primer is dry on doors — seal floating panels with urethanized caulk (eliminates black lines, cracks, and seams)
49. Some drawers need same treatment, some don't (if drawer fronts have panels/detail, treat like doors)
50. Same caulking on bases — seal all visible seams, cracks, and lines exposed by the primer
51. **Context:** builder-grade cabinets come with stain and clear coat (dark finish hides these gaps). When you paint them a lighter color, every crack and seam shows. We fix what the builders left behind.
52. **New hardware (first-time install):** drill holes AFTER primer dries but BEFORE final coat — drilling after final coat would damage the finish
53. Lightly sand all primer on doors — everything: detail, sides, grooves. Use "dead" 220 grit (killed against a rough surface so it has no grit, just smooths the profile)
54. Blow with compressor nozzle, wipe with damp rag, set on drying racks
55. Same dead-sand technique on bases, wipe down, caulk any remaining visible lines

---

## PHASE 12 — FINAL COAT (Topcoat)

56. **Doors & drawers:** same assembly line process as priming — one by one, pass to sprayer, spray, rack, repeat
57. **Vertical spraying cuts process time in half** compared to laying doors flat
58. Let doors and drawers dry overnight
59. Spray bases
60. Let bases dry overnight
61. Do NOT remove any masking — let everything cure properly before handling

---

## PHASE 13 — RETURN & REASSEMBLY

62. Return next day (or whenever appropriate — timing varies by job)
63. Check bases for runs, imperfections — touch up as needed
64. Remove all masking inside kitchen bases
65. **Deep clean inside of all bases** — every interior surface cleaned and ready for customer to put belongings back
66. Set doors face down on table with plastic
67. Install hinges — **soft-close hinge upgrade included in every job** (VPP sources Everbilt 35mm face frame hinges from Home Depot, cost absorbed into $150/door pricing. Doors never slam again.)
68. **Handle audit (every job):** regardless of whether installing new handles, patch ALL existing hardware holes and drill new straight ones. Builders install handles crooked and hide it with dark stain. VPP corrects this — included in the $150/door price.
69. Install hardware (new or reused, or none if no hardware)
69. Follow numerical order — match hinge hole numbers to base opening tape
70. Remove tape from inside bases as each door/drawer is installed
71. **Apply felt pads** — one on top and one on bottom inside face of every door and drawer (prevents door finish from sticking to base finish over time)
72. **Align all doors** — adjust every single hinge so all doors line up and match perfectly

---

## PHASE 14 — FINAL DEEP CLEAN & HANDOFF

73. Deep clean countertops
74. Deep clean floors
75. Take after pictures of completed kitchen cabinets
76. Kitchen is ready — customer can begin putting belongings back

---

## DIFFERENTIATORS EMBEDDED IN THIS PROCESS

- **Electric sanders, not hand sanding** — proper scuffing for real adhesion
- **Every surface sanded including grooves and detail** — most painters skip this, leading to peeling within months
- **Vertical spraying with hook system** — cuts process time in half
- **Two-person assembly line** — efficient, consistent flow
- **Caulking all seams and floating panels** — eliminates the cracks builders leave behind
- **Degreaser deep clean on cabinet tops** — the area everyone ignores
- **Deep clean of cabinet interiors** — customer gets a clean kitchen back, not just a painted one
- **Felt pads on every door and drawer** — prevents finish-to-finish sticking
- **Soft-close hinge upgrade included** — baked into $150/door pricing, no separate line item
- **Handle audit on every job** — patch all old crooked holes, redrill straight ones, included in price
- **Hinge hole numbering system** — hook drill holes are always hidden after reinstall
- **Festool filter for dust/fume containment** — professional-grade air quality during the job
