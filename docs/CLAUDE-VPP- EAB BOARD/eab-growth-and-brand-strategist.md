## Section 1: Conversion-Focused Website Architecture for Home Services Contractors

### 1.1 Above-the-Fold Hero Requirements

The hero section must accomplish three tasks before the visitor scrolls: confirm the service, confirm the location, confirm the next step ([Hook Agency, 2026](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/)). Every element that requires a visitor to hunt for information before those three confirmations is a conversion leak.

**Required hero elements (in priority order):**

| Element | Specification | Valley Painting Pros Application |
|---|---|---|
| H1 Headline | Names the service + location. Benefit-driven, not clever. | "East Valley Cabinet Refinishing & Interior Painting — Licensed, Bonded, Insured" |
| Subheadline | Confirms delivery mechanism + credibility signal | "ROC Licensed · $1M Coverage · 5-Star Rated · Free On-Site Estimate" |
| Primary CTA | Action-oriented, specific — not "Submit" or "Contact Us" | "Get My Free Estimate" or "Schedule Your Free Estimate" |
| Trust bar | ROC number, star rating, review count, badges — immediately below CTA | "ROC #[XXXXXX] · 4.9★ Google (87 Reviews) · Licensed & Bonded in AZ" |
| Hero image/video | Real job photography — before/after, crew in uniform, branded vehicle | Cabinet refinishing transformation or exterior job with branded truck |
| Phone number | Tap-to-call in header; visible without scrolling on mobile | Click-to-call in sticky header |

**Hero form vs. CTA button decision rule** (derived from [YouTube analysis of 500+ hero sections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7AL6slFxXyc)):

- **Form in hero** → Use when: high-intent traffic (Google Search), simple service, short form (name + phone + service type + zip code only)
- **CTA button → estimate page** → Use when: low-intent traffic (Meta ads, Nextdoor), multi-service offering, premium positioning requiring education before contact
- **Both** → Use when: service is urgent (emergency water damage), visitor volume is split between high and low intent

For Valley Painting Pros running Meta ads as primary acquisition: **CTA button → estimate request page** is correct. Reserve form-in-hero for Google LSA landing pages.

> **Mobile-first specification:** 64% of global web traffic is mobile ([Hook Agency, 2026](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/)). Google data shows 53% of mobile users abandon pages taking longer than 3 seconds. Every additional second of load time reduces conversions ~32%. The hero must be fully rendered, CTA visible, phone tappable — all above the fold on a 390px-wide screen — before the visitor scrolls.

### 1.2 Three Proven Section-Sequencing Models

#### Model A: Hero → Problem → Solution → Process → Proof → Guarantee → CTA
*The StoryBrand-aligned contractor homepage sequence*

This is the most widely deployed sequence for home services because it mirrors the homeowner's decision journey: they arrive with a problem, they need to know you understand it, then they need a reason to choose you over others.

| Section | Content Function | Valley Painting Pros Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero** | Confirm service, location, next step | "The East Valley's Cabinet Refinishing Specialists — Free Estimate" |
| **Problem** | Name the pain that brought them here | "Your kitchen cabinets are dated — but full replacement costs $20,000+" |
| **Solution / Offer** | Your specific solution to their specific problem | "Cabinet Refinishing: Professional results in 2–3 days for a fraction of replacement cost" |
| **Process** | 3-step plan (reduces anxiety, lowers effort perception) | "1. Free on-site estimate → 2. We prep, prime & refinish in 2–3 days → 3. You love it or we fix it" |
| **Proof** | Before/after photos, reviews, named testimonials | Gallery + Google review embeds + video testimonial |
| **Guarantee** | Risk reversal — what if it goes wrong? | "100% Satisfaction Guarantee. If you're not happy, we make it right — no arguments, no extra charges." |
| **Trust Signals** | ROC number, insurance, bond, awards | License badge block with verifiable ROC number |
| **Final CTA** | Same action as hero, slightly different wording | "Ready for a kitchen transformation? Get your free estimate today." |

**When to use:** Primary homepage and cabinet refinishing service page when traffic is cold (Meta ads, Nextdoor referrals, door hangers scanning a QR code).

**When NOT to use:** Google Search landing pages where intent is already high — shorten to Hero → Process → Proof → CTA. Skip the problem education.

#### Model B: EPRC — Exposition → Process → Results → Call to Action
*Simpler structure for service pages; strong for SEO content*

Originally documented in landing page architecture communities, EPRC is best for individual service pages rather than homepages ([Reddit web_design community, 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/web_design/comments/1luoxva/this_is_for_people_who_dont_know_what_sections_to/)):

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Exposition** | Define what the service is, who it's for, when it's needed |
| **Process** | How you deliver it (step-by-step) |
| **Results** | Before/after, testimonials, project photos, metrics |
| **CTA** | Single action with no competing links |

**Valley Painting Pros application (Cabinet Refinishing Service Page):**
- E: "Cabinet refinishing is the professional process of sanding, priming, and spray-applying a factory-grade finish to your existing cabinet boxes and doors — without tearing out your kitchen."
- P: Day 1 – Prep and hardware removal. Day 2 – Prime, first coat. Day 3 – Final coat, reinstall, inspection.
- R: [Before/after gallery] + "Saved $14,000 vs. Home Depot replacement estimate — Scottsdale homeowner"
- CTA: "Get Your Free Cabinet Refinishing Estimate"

**When to use:** Individual service pages with SEO intent. Works well paired with Model A homepage.

#### Model C: Conversion-Centered Design (CCD) — Unbounce Framework
*Creator: Oli Gardner / Unbounce; 7 principles*

The [Unbounce CCD framework](https://unbounce.com/conversion-centered-design/) structures pages around seven principles in this order of priority:

1. **Encapsulation** — Focus attention by containing the conversion goal
2. **Contrast and Color** — CTA button color must stand out from everything else on the page
3. **Directional Cues** — Arrows, eyeline direction in photos, whitespace all point to the CTA
4. **Whitespace** — Breathing room creates perceived importance
5. **Urgency and Scarcity** — Ethical time limits or slot availability
6. **Trust** — Testimonials, badges, reviews placed near the CTA (not buried in a separate section)
7. **Benefit-Focused Copy** — Every element serves the decision, nothing decorates

**Valley Painting Pros worked example (Lead Ads landing page):**
- Encapsulation: Single column, no navigation links away from the page
- Contrast: Orange CTA button on white background (high contrast, brand color)
- Directional: Customer testimonial photo with eyeline facing the estimate form
- Whitespace: Large margins around the form to signal it's the most important element
- Trust: ROC license badge, insurance amount, star rating — all placed directly above the form

**When to use:** Dedicated landing pages for paid traffic (Meta Lead Ads linking to website). When you cannot use Instant Forms and send traffic to a page.

### 1.3 Trust-Signal Hierarchy for Licensed Arizona Contractors

Based on [Hook Agency's practitioner research](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/) and [Nielsen Norman Group's 4 credibility factors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/), trust signals have a defined hierarchy of impact for home services:

| Rank | Trust Signal | Placement | Arizona-Specific Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Google Reviews (count + star rating)** | Hero section, near every CTA | Link to Google Business Profile. Reviewers are real neighbors. |
| 2 | **Before/after photography (real jobs)** | Hero, proof section, gallery page | Cabinets and interiors — same angle, same lighting |
| 3 | **ROC License Number (displayed + linked)** | Hero trust bar, footer | Link to [roc.az.gov/contractor-search](https://azroc.my.site.com/AZRoc/s/contractor-search) so homeowners can verify. AZ law requires ROC number on all advertising per [AZAGC](https://azagc.org/industry-resources/contractor-licensing-arizona). |
| 4 | **Insurance amount ($1M/$2M GL)** | Trust bar, about page | Specific dollar amounts convert better than just "insured" |
| 5 | **Bond status** | About page, trust bar | "Bonded — AZ Contractor's Bond on file with the ROC" |
| 6 | **Workmanship guarantee (written, specific)** | Guarantee section, near CTA | See Section 4 for guarantee structures |
| 7 | **Named testimonials with photo + location** | Proof section, near final CTA | "Sarah M., Chandler, AZ — Cabinet Refinishing" |
| 8 | **Owner photo and name** | About page, hero video | Accountability signal; homeowners want to know who is responsible |
| 9 | **Crew uniforms and branded vehicle photos** | Gallery, process section | Signal professionalism without saying "we're professional" |
| 10 | **Warranty terms (specific timeline)** | Guarantee section | "2-year workmanship warranty, in writing" |

**Arizona ROC License context:** Arizona is one of the strictest contractor licensing states. The ROC requires all contracting work valued over $1,000 to be performed by a licensed contractor ([CheckLicensed, 2026](https://checklicensed.com/blog/how-to-check-contractor-license-arizona)). For painting in Arizona, the ROC offers three classifications: **R-34** (residential only), **C-34** (commercial only), and **CR-34** (dual residential and commercial). Valley Painting Pros holds **CR-34**, which covers both residential and commercial Painting and Wall Covering work — a stronger credibility signal than R-34 alone because it requires the broader competency standard. Homeowners can verify any contractor's license, bond status, expiration date, and complaint history at [roc.az.gov](https://azroc.my.site.com/AZRoc/s/contractor-search). This lookup capability makes your ROC number on your website a direct, verifiable trust signal — not just a claim.

> **Leveraging the ROC:** Add to your site: *"Verify our license: ROC #[XXXXXX] — Licensed since [year], zero complaints. Check us at roc.az.gov"* This is a pattern interrupt because almost no local painting contractor does it. It signals confidence.

### 1.4 Form vs. Phone vs. Estimate-Request Friction Tradeoffs

| Contact Method | Best For | Friction Level | Lead Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Click-to-call (phone)** | Urgent jobs, Google LSA traffic, older demographic | Lowest friction | Highest quality (high intent) |
| **Estimate request form (3–5 fields)** | Meta ad traffic, Nextdoor, mid-intent | Medium | Medium-high |
| **Meta Instant Form** | Cold Meta traffic, no website required | Lowest (stays in app) | Variable (lower than web form) |
| **Full contact form (7+ fields)** | Never recommended for home services | Highest | Leads that do convert are qualified, but most abandon |
| **Chat widget** | Research-phase visitors; educational | Low | Low-medium |

**Recommended configuration for Valley Painting Pros:**
- Sticky header: phone number (tap-to-call) + "Get Free Estimate" button — visible on every page
- Above-the-fold CTA: "Get My Free Estimate" → leads to 4-field form: Name, Phone, Service Type (dropdown: Interior Painting / Exterior Painting / Cabinet Refinishing / Other), Zip Code
- After-hours: auto-text response — "Thanks for reaching out to Valley Painting Pros! We'll call you within 1 business hour. Your ROC-licensed estimate is always free."

## Section 2: Brand Voice and Positioning

### 2.1 Terminology That Signals Quality

Vocabulary choices communicate positioning before price is ever mentioned. In painting and cabinet work, word choice separates commodity contractors from craft-oriented businesses.

| Lower-Quality Signal | Higher-Quality Signal | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| "Painters" | **"Craftsmen"** or **"Finishing Specialists"** | Trade vocabulary signals mastery |
| "We paint cabinets" | **"We refinish cabinets"** | "Refinish" implies breaking down and rebuilding the finish system; "paint" implies coating over |
| "We do a good job" | **"Factory-quality spray finish"** | Specificity of process signals expertise |
| "Free estimate" | **"Complimentary on-site consultation"** | Premium frame; still free |
| "We'll paint your house" | **"We'll restore and protect your home's exterior"** | Outcome + benefit, not task |
| "Cheap prices" | **"Transparent, fixed pricing"** | Value without race to bottom |
| "Quick and affordable" | **"Efficient and fairly priced — with a 2-year workmanship warranty"** | Speed + protection, not just speed + cheap |

**Refinishing vs. Painting terminology distinction** (critical for cabinet work):
The practitioner community makes this distinction explicitly: "Refinishing suggests breaking the old finish and providing a new one... some are painters, some are finishers" ([DIY Cabinet Refinishing Facebook Group practitioner discussion, 2023](https://www.facebook.com/groups/diycabinetrefinishing/posts/1619124501952379/)). By calling the service "cabinet refinishing" rather than "cabinet painting," Valley Painting Pros:
1. Commands higher price expectation
2. Attracts customers who understand they're hiring a specialist
3. Differentiates from handymen who "roll some paint on"
4. Aligns with the higher-search-intent keyword (see Section 8)

### 2.2 Premium vs. Budget Positioning Language

**Budget contractor language patterns:** "Affordable," "competitive," "low cost," "free estimates," "best price"

**Premium contractor language patterns:** "Investment," "craftsmanship," "factory-grade finish," "professional-grade materials," "warranty-backed," "licensed specialists," "fixed price — no surprises"

Premium positioning does not require being the most expensive. It requires being specific. Vague claims like "quality work" and "excellent service" are noise — every competitor says them and they create zero differentiation ([BrandCraft, 2025](https://www.brandcraft.com/blog/breaking-through-the-noise-how-home-services-companies-can-stand-out-in-a-crowded-market)).

**Specific differentiators Valley Painting Pros should name:**
- ROC license classification (CR-34, dual residential/commercial) — most homeowners don't know their painter needs a specific license, and dual classification signals broader competency
- $1M/$2M insurance — specific amounts mean something; "insured" is meaningless
- Spray application for cabinets vs. brush/roll — factory-grade finish
- Surface prep process (sand, clean, prime before topcoat)
- Written warranty terms (specific years)

### 2.3 Brand Archetypes for Residential Painting and Cabinet Refinishing

Three archetypes dominate home services marketing. Each attracts different customers and commands different positioning:

| Archetype | Core Message | Target Buyer | Best For | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Local/Community** | "Your East Valley neighbor, not a franchise" | Middle-income homeowners who distrust corporations; older demographic | Nextdoor, Facebook groups, door hangers, yard signs | Competing on high-end projects where prestige matters more than belonging |
| **Family Business** | "Valley Painting Pros — [Name] & family, serving the East Valley since [year]" | Homeowners who want accountability; suburban buyers | Owner-photo campaigns, Meta video ads, Google review responses | Scaling to multi-crew if family narrative becomes inauthentic |
| **Craftsman/Specialist** | "Cabinet refinishing specialists — not generalists" | Value-conscious but quality-aware buyers; homeowners who've had bad experiences with low-bid contractors | Cabinet refinishing wedge service; premium exterior; Meta ads with process video | Pure price-comparison shoppers (they'll always go cheaper) |

**Recommended Valley Painting Pros positioning synthesis:**
> **"The East Valley's cabinet refinishing craftsmen — family-owned, ROC licensed, satisfaction guaranteed. Serving Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe, Scottsdale, and Queen Creek."**

This blends Craftsman (specialist signal) + Family (accountability signal) + Local (Arizona-specific) + Trust signals (ROC, guarantee). It works across all channels.

### 3.2 PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Structure:**
1. **Problem:** Identify and name the specific pain point
2. **Agitate:** Intensify the problem — make it feel more real, more costly, more urgent
3. **Solution:** Present your offer as the specific remedy

**Cabinet Refinishing Worked Example:**

> **Problem:** Your kitchen cabinets look dated — and every time someone walks into your kitchen, you notice it.
>
> **Agitate:** Full cabinet replacement runs $15,000–$25,000. And they want you to live without a kitchen for 3–6 weeks. Cheaper options? Stick-on films that peel within a year, or a "painter" who brushes on a latex coat that chips every time you open a cabinet door.
>
> **Solution:** Valley Painting Pros refinishes your cabinets with a factory-grade spray finish — same prep process used by cabinet manufacturers. Done in 2–3 days. Fixed price. 2-year warranty. ROC licensed and insured in Arizona.
>
> **CTA:** Get your free, no-pressure on-site estimate →

**When to use PAS:** Meta ad body copy targeting homeowners 35–65 who have expressed interest in home improvement; email follow-up sequences; Nextdoor posts.

**When NOT to use:** As the primary structure for audiences who are not yet aware they have a problem (very cold traffic). Use AIDA for those.

### 3.3 AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Structure:**
1. **Attention:** Stop the scroll — image, headline, unexpected statistic
2. **Interest:** Build curiosity with a relevant fact or story
3. **Desire:** Show the transformation / outcomes they want
4. **Action:** Single, clear next step

**Interior Painting Worked Example (Meta Ad):**

> **Attention (image hook):** Split-screen: left = beige walls from 2009; right = bold, professionally painted living room.
>
> **Attention (headline):** "Phoenix homeowners: Your walls haven't changed since Obama was president. We can fix that."
>
> **Interest:** "Most paint jobs fade, chalk, and yellow within 5–7 years. The color and sheen in your home right now? Probably past its prime."
>
> **Desire:** "[Homeowner name] had her entire main floor repainted in one day. 'I can't believe it's the same house,' she said. [Before/after photo]"
>
> **Action:** "Get a free estimate from Valley Painting Pros — ROC licensed, $1M insured, serving Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe, Scottsdale, and Queen Creek. Tap 'Get Quote' →"

**When to use:** Full ad sequences, especially Meta cold traffic where awareness is being built. Works well in lead ad format because it warms the prospect before the form appears.

### 3.4 Before-After-Bridge (BAB)

**Structure:**
1. **Before:** Current painful state (be specific, not generic)
2. **After:** Ideal future state (sensory, emotional, concrete)
3. **Bridge:** How to get from Before to After (your service)

**Cabinet Refinishing Worked Example (Short-form social post):**

> **Before:** Honey oak cabinets. Dated hardware. A kitchen you avoid showing company.
>
> **After:** Clean, bright, modern finish. The kitchen friends actually comment on. The one you feel proud of.
>
> **Bridge:** Valley Painting Pros cabinet refinishing. 2–3 days. Fixed price. No mess, no chaos, no $20K remodel. ROC licensed. Free estimate this week.

**When to use:** Instagram Reels captions, Facebook posts, before/after carousel copy, email subject lines. Perfect for organic content because it's short and visual.

**When NOT to use:** Long landing pages or situations where the audience doesn't yet understand that refinishing exists as an option (needs more education first).

### 3.5 StoryBrand SB7 Framework

**Creator:** Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand* (HarperCollins, 2017)

**The seven components** ([StoryBrand Framework summary](https://resultsandco.com.au/blog/the-storybrand-framework)):

| # | Element | Core Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Character** (the Hero) | What does your customer want? |
| 2 | **Has a problem** | External, internal, philosophical problem |
| 3 | **Meets a guide** (you) | Who has authority + empathy? |
| 4 | **Who gives them a plan** | What simple 3-step plan removes risk? |
| 5 | **And calls them to action** | Direct and transitional CTAs |
| 6 | **That helps them avoid failure** | What bad outcome are they escaping? |
| 7 | **And ends in success** | What does winning look like? |

**Critical insight:** The customer is the hero. Valley Painting Pros is the guide (not the hero). This is counterintuitive but essential. When your website talks about "Our 20 years of experience" and "Our commitment to quality," you've made yourself the hero. The customer doesn't care about you — they care about their problem and their transformation.

**Valley Painting Pros SB7 Brand Script:**

| Element | Valley Painting Pros Application |
|---|---|
| Character | East Valley homeowner who loves their home but feels stuck with dated cabinets or outdated paint — and doesn't want to blow $20K on a full remodel |
| Problem | **External:** Dated cabinets, peeling paint, failed DIY. **Internal:** Embarrassment when guests visit; anxiety about hiring the wrong contractor. **Philosophical:** "I deserve a home I'm proud of — without being ripped off." |
| Guide | Valley Painting Pros — ROC licensed craftsmen who've refinished [X] kitchens across the East Valley (Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe, Scottsdale, Queen Creek) and back every job with a written guarantee |
| Plan | 1. Schedule your free on-site estimate. 2. We prep, prime, and refinish in 2–3 days. 3. Love your kitchen — guaranteed. |
| Call to Action | "Get My Free Estimate" (direct CTA) / "See Our Work" (transitional CTA) |
| Failure | Wasting $20K on a remodel they didn't need. Hiring an unlicensed "painter" who charges less but delivers a peeling mess. |
| Success | A kitchen that looks professionally refinished. Guests who notice and ask who did it. Money left for the vacation they actually wanted. |

**When to use:** Primary website homepage structure, About page narrative, video script for brand video, Meta video ad script.

### 3.6 4Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push

**Structure:**
1. **Promise:** State the single, specific outcome you deliver
2. **Picture:** Describe what that outcome looks and feels like (sensory)
3. **Proof:** Evidence it's real (before/after, testimonial, stats)
4. **Push:** Urgency + CTA

**Cabinet Refinishing Email Example:**

> **Promise:** I can transform your dated kitchen cabinets into a clean, modern finish — without the $20,000 remodel price tag or 6 weeks without a kitchen.
>
> **Picture:** Picture opening your kitchen cabinets in the morning — smooth, bright, perfectly finished doors that still smell of fresh paint. Your counters haven't moved. Your layout hasn't changed. But the kitchen looks like it belongs in a model home.
>
> **Proof:** We've completed 47 cabinet refinishing jobs across the East Valley this year alone. Our average project costs $2,800–$4,500 and takes 2–3 days. Here's what Sarah in Chandler said: "I've gotten more compliments on my kitchen in the last week than in the 8 years I've lived here."
>
> **Push:** We have 3 estimate slots open this week in your area. After that, the wait is 3 weeks. Tap here to claim your free estimate.

**When to use:** Email campaigns, direct mail copy, longer Meta ad body copy for warm retargeting audiences.

## Section 4: Offer Construction and Risk Reversal

### 4.1 The Hormozi Value Equation

**Creator:** Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (Acquisition.com, 2021)

**The equation:**
$$\text{Value} = \frac{\text{Dream Outcome} \times \text{Perceived Likelihood of Achievement}}{\text{Time Delay} \times \text{Effort \& Sacrifice}}$$

**Four levers explained** ([MobiGym / Hormozi framework breakdown, 2025](https://www.mobigym.lu/blog/our-blog-1/the-hormozi-value-equation-unlocking-extraordinary-business-value-18)):

| Lever | Direction to Maximize Value | Valley Painting Pros Application |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | Increase | Don't sell "painted cabinets" — sell "a kitchen your friends ask about and you're proud to show" |
| Perceived Likelihood | Increase | ROC license, before/afters, written warranty, specific number of jobs completed, Google reviews |
| Time Delay | Decrease | "Done in 2–3 days" vs. competitors who schedule 4–6 weeks out |
| Effort & Sacrifice | Decrease | "We handle everything — you don't lift a finger. We even clean up completely." |

**The Grand Slam Offer structure** for cabinet refinishing:

A Grand Slam Offer is defined as one that "cannot be compared to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee" ([Greg Faxon's $100M Offers Summary, 2024](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary)).

*Valley Painting Pros Cabinet Refinishing Grand Slam Offer (worked example):*

> **Core offer:** Full kitchen cabinet refinishing — factory-grade spray finish, all prep included
> **Bonus 1:** New hardware consultation included (we'll show you what pulls work with your new finish — value $150)
> **Bonus 2:** Touch-up kit provided (matching paint for future minor repairs — value $75)
> **Guarantee:** 100% satisfaction or we return and fix it at no charge. Period.
> **Urgency:** "We book 3 weeks in advance. We have [X] slots available for projects starting this month."
> **Price anchor:** "Cabinet replacement: $15,000–$25,000 at Home Depot. Full remodel: $20,000–$50,000. Our refinishing: $2,800–$4,500."

**Hormozi rule on discounting:** "Whenever trying to close a deal, never discount the main offer. It teaches customers your prices are negotiable. Adding bonuses to increase value to close the deal is far superior to cutting prices." ([Greg Faxon, 2024](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary)). Apply this: if a prospect pushes back on price, add touch-up kit + hardware consultation, don't reduce the price.

### 4.2 Guarantee Structures Used by Top Contractors

Guarantees are the most powerful risk-reversal tool in home services because the primary buying objection is *"What if something goes wrong?"* Arizona contractor licensing provides backstop consumer protection (the ROC Workmanship Fund), but you should have your own guarantee layer as a marketing signal.

| Guarantee Type | Structure | Credibility Requirement | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Satisfaction Guarantee** | "100% happy or we fix it free" | Trust in your processes + return visit capacity | Broad positioning; cabinet refinishing |
| **Fixed-Price Guarantee** | "Quote is the price — no surprise charges" | Accurate estimating, signed scope-of-work | Eliminates #1 contractor complaint |
| **Workmanship Warranty (2-year)** | "Any defect in application is fixed free within 2 years" | Documentation, per-job photo records | All residential painting; cabinet refinishing |
| **On-Time Guarantee** | "We start on the day we say, or we discount X%" | Tight scheduling system, buffer calendar | High-trust positioning; premium customers |
| **No-Mess Guarantee** | "We protect every surface. If we damage anything, we fix it or replace it." | Proper prep materials, drop cloths, masking | Cabinet refinishing (overspray risk) |
| **Lifetime Workmanship** | "As long as you own the home, application defects are fixed free" | Strong confidence in process; risk is actually low | Advanced positioning; significant marketing power |

**Worked example — Valley Painting Pros Guarantee Stack:**

> *Our 3-Part Promise:*
> *1. Fixed Price: Your estimate is your final price. We never add charges you didn't approve.*
> *2. No-Mess Guarantee: We protect everything we don't paint. Zero cleanup left for you.*
> *3. Satisfaction Warranty: Any defect in our workmanship within 2 years? We return and fix it at no cost to you.*

**What Green Mountain Painters (Vermont) does:** They offer an explicit 3-year express written warranty with specific carve-outs (horizontal surfaces, natural stains excluded), which is more credible than vague "lifetime" language because it shows expertise and specificity ([Green Mountain Painters warranty page](https://www.vtpainters.com/warranty-guarantees/)).

**On "lifetime" warranties:** The practitioner community is explicit that "lifetime warranties from painting contractors are often functionally meaningless" ([Green Mountain Painters, 2023](https://www.vtpainters.com/warranty-guarantees/)). A specific 2-year or 3-year written warranty with clear inclusions/exclusions is more credible than "lifetime" language with no definition.

### 4.3 Bundling, Price Anchors, Urgency Without Gimmick

**Price anchoring for cabinet refinishing:**
Always state the comparison price first. The sequence: "Replacement: $15,000–$25,000 → Refinishing: $2,800–$4,500" creates massive perceived value. The anchor is set before the price is revealed. This is fundamental price psychology (anchoring bias, Kahneman).

**Bundle options (increase average ticket without racing to bottom):**

| Bundle Name | Includes | Price Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen Refresh | Cabinet refinishing + interior painting of kitchen walls | Price as one package; slight discount vs. separate |
| Move-In Ready | Interior painting (main floor) + cabinet refinishing | Strong for new homeowners — "make it yours" |
| Full Interior Transform | All interior painting + cabinets + trim | Premium tier; 3-week lead time |

**Urgency without gimmick:**
The key is *real* scarcity. A 2-truck crew genuinely can only do a limited number of jobs per month. Ethical urgency:
- "We have 3 estimate slots available this week in Chandler/Gilbert — after that, you're looking at a 3-week wait."
- "We're finishing a cabinet job in your neighborhood this Thursday. If you'd like an estimate while we're nearby, we can often start sooner."
- Seasonal: "Spring is our busiest booking season in Phoenix — summer heat affects cure times for exterior work."

**Never use:** Fake countdown timers, fake "only 2 spots left" claims that reset every visit. These are visible as gimmicks to modern homeowners and destroy trust exactly when trust is what you're building.

### 6.5 Door Hangers and Direct Mail Integration with Digital

**The Proximity Marketing System** (practitioner-developed framework):

Every completed job is a marketing asset for the surrounding neighborhood. The system:

1. **During job:** Place yard sign with company name, phone, ROC number
2. **During job:** 20–30 door hangers to immediately adjacent homes: *"We're painting/refinishing your neighbor's [cabinets/home] this week. Want a quote while we're in the neighborhood? We can often start sooner."*
3. **After job:** Proximity direct mail to a ¼-mile radius: postcard with before/after photo, specific address reference ("We just completed a cabinet refinishing 2 blocks from you"), and offer
4. **Digital amplification:** Run geofenced Meta or Google Display ads targeting a ½-mile radius around the completed job site for 2 weeks post-completion

This creates a "surround sound" effect — the homeowner sees the truck, gets the door hanger, receives the postcard, and sees the digital ad. Multiple impressions build familiarity before they're ever in market ([Pipeline On, 2024](https://pipelineon.com/how-to/market-to-neighbors/); [LeadTruffle, 2026](https://www.leadtruffle.co/blog/painting-contractor-marketing-ideas-book-more-jobs-2026/)).

**Door hanger design requirements:**
- Company name + phone (large, readable from arm's length)
- ROC license number (Arizona law requires on advertising)
- Single offer (not a list of all services — one job, one message)
- Before/after photo if possible
- QR code linking to estimate request page (track scans by batch/neighborhood)
- "Painting your neighbor's home this week" hook for jobs-in-progress hangers

**Response rate benchmarks:** Door hangers typically generate 0.5–2% response rate. At 100 hangers per job and 1% response, that's 1 additional lead per job at a cost of ~$25–$50 in printing. Given a $3,000+ average job value, the ROI math is excellent even at the low end.

## Section 7: Visual Design Principles for Ads and Landing Pages

### 7.1 Visual Hierarchy for Service Businesses

Visual hierarchy is the arrangement of elements so viewers instantly understand their order of importance ([IxDF, 2016](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/visual-hierarchy)). For home services landing pages, the correct hierarchy is:

**Correct eye-path:** Headline → Supporting claim → Visual (before/after or hero photo) → Social proof → CTA button

The CTA button must be **the most visually prominent interactive element on the page** — not the logo, not the hero image ([Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-high-converting-landing-page-design)).

**Hierarchy tools:**

| Tool | Application |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Headlines larger than body copy; CTA button large enough to tap on mobile |
| **Color contrast** | CTA button in brand accent color (e.g., orange) on white/light background — high contrast |
| **Whitespace** | Surround the CTA and key messages with empty space to signal importance |
| **Directional cues** | Customer testimonial photo where subject's gaze points toward the form/CTA |
| **Typography weight** | Bold headlines, regular body, italic for quotes — never more than 2 font families |

**Typography norms for home services:**
- Choose 2 fonts: 1 for headlines (slightly bold, distinctive), 1 for body copy (highly readable)
- Body copy minimum 16px on mobile ([SiteGround Academy, 2025](https://www.siteground.com/academy/visual-hierarchy/))
- High contrast: dark text on white background (or white on dark) — avoid gray on gray
- Trust signal text (ROC number, insurance, reviews) — use a slightly smaller size but keep readable

**Color guidelines for home services trust:**
- **Blue:** Trust, reliability (safe for corporate feel; less personal)
- **Orange/Red:** Energy, urgency, action (good for CTA buttons — high attention)
- **Green:** Growth, go, approval (good for "Get Started" or "Book" buttons; connotes permission)
- **White + dark text:** Cleanliness, professionalism — signals quality for premium positioning
- **Avoid:** Neon colors, overly complex color palettes (3+ brand colors creates visual chaos)

### 7.2 Before/After Photography Standards

Before/after photography is the primary conversion asset for cabinet refinishing and painting. Standards to maintain:

**Technical requirements** ([Moonshine Design, 2025](https://themoonshinedesign.com/2025/01/26/capture-before-after-photos-like-pro-a-guide-for-log-home-contractors/)):
- Same camera angle and position (mark the spot; use tape on the floor)
- Same focal length (no wide-angle on "after" to make space appear larger)
- Same time of day for consistent lighting
- Remove all clutter, staging, and construction debris from "after" shot
- Minimum 300 PPI resolution for print; shoot in RAW if using DSLR
- Use a tripod for consistency
- For interiors: control eye height (keep camera at counter height for kitchens)

**Best practices for cabinet refinishing before/afters** ([Full Scope Creative, 2025](https://fullscopecreative.com/before-and-after-photos-for-contractors/); [Modernize, 2018](https://modernize.com/contractor-resources/articles/why-before-and-after-photos-are-so-important-and-how-to-take-yours)):
- Shoot the full kitchen (wide shot) AND close-up detail shots (hardware, door edge, hinge area)
- Document problem areas specifically — "before" chipping, delamination, dated finish
- Include a brief caption: "Customer: Chandler AZ. Service: Cabinet refinishing (full kitchen). Time: 3 days. Cost: $3,450."
- Stage the "after" shot: clear counters, a simple bowl of fruit or coffee maker, fresh kitchen towel
- Create a habit: photograph every job before work begins, even if the project seems ordinary

**Key psychological insight:** Cabinet refinishing buyers are not searching for the cheapest option — they already found a cheaper option (DIY paint, RTA replacement) and rejected it. They're searching for "the right option." Position accordingly: not cheapest, but best value for the investment.

### 10.1 Google Business Profile Optimization Framework

**Framework name:** RDP Local Visibility System

**Creator / origin:** Google Business Profile local ranking guidance. Google states that local results are primarily based on relevance, distance, and prominence, and that complete business information helps Google match profiles to relevant searches ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Google also states that review count and review score can factor into local ranking, with more reviews and positive ratings able to improve local ranking ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)).

**Core structure:**

| Pillar | What it means | VPP operating rule |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well the profile matches what the searcher wants | Categories, services, description, photos, Q&A, and website service pages must reinforce "painting contractor," "cabinet refinishing," "interior painting," and "exterior painting." |
| Distance | How close the business is to the searcher or searched location | Service area should reflect actual East Valley coverage (Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe, Scottsdale, Queen Creek), not an inflated statewide or metro-wide footprint. Build suburb-specific proof assets instead of pretending distance does not matter. |
| Prominence | How well-known and trusted the business appears across Google and the web | Reviews, review responses, links, citations, before/after photos, ROC license mentions, and local project references build prominence. |

**Optimization sequence for Valley Painting Pros:**

1. **Profile integrity first:** Use the real-world business name, accurate service area, real phone number, real website, and customer-facing hours. Google says Business Profile names should reflect the real-world name used on signage, website, stationery, and by customers, and service-area businesses should use one profile for the central office or location with a designated service area ([Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)).
2. **Category hierarchy:** Primary category should be the closest available match to the core business, such as "Painter" or "Painting contractor" depending on available GBP category options. Secondary categories should only be used when they are accurate, because Google says categories should describe what the business is, not every product or service it has ([Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)).
3. **Service menu buildout:** Add separate services for cabinet refinishing, interior painting, exterior painting, drywall repair if offered, trim painting, door painting, garage floor coatings if offered, and color consultation if offered. Each service should use homeowner language plus proof, such as "cabinet refinishing with sprayed finish, prep, primer, and hardware reinstall."
4. **Photo system:** Upload real project photos by service type: cabinet before/after, exterior before/after, crew in uniform, prep/masking, final walkthrough, branded vehicle, and owner/estimator. Google says adding photos and videos of the business, products, and services can help complete the profile and make it more attractive to customers ([Google Business Profile photo guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)).
5. **Post cadence:** Publish one GBP post per week during active growth seasons. Rotate "recent project," "cabinet transformation," "seasonal exterior reminder," "review spotlight," and "estimate availability."
6. **Q&A seeding:** Add and answer common questions from the owner account or through customer-facing prompts: "Are you licensed and insured in Arizona?", "Do you refinish cabinets or replace them?", "How long does cabinet refinishing take?", "Do you serve Gilbert / Mesa / Chandler / Tempe / Scottsdale / Queen Creek?"
7. **Review engine:** Connect GBP to the review acquisition system in Section 10.2. GBP is not optimized until reviews are being requested, answered, and repurposed weekly.
8. **Tracking:** Use UTM-tagged website links for GBP, track calls from GBP separately where possible, and log leads as "GBP call," "GBP website click," "GBP message," or "Google Maps."

**Framework name:** Earn, Ask, Route, Respond, Repurpose Review Flywheel

**Creator / origin:** Google Business Profile review guidance plus service-business operating practice. Google says businesses can ask customers for reviews by sharing a review request link or QR code, but reviews must reflect a genuine experience and incentives for reviews are prohibited ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en)). Google also says merchants may solicit genuine reviews without incentives or attempts to influence the rating or contents of the review, but may not selectively solicit only positive reviews or discourage negative reviews ([Google Maps contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)).

**Core structure:**

| Step | Purpose | VPP operating rule |
|---|---|---|
| Earn | Create a review-worthy moment before asking | Final walkthrough must end with a clean jobsite, confirmed scope completion, and customer approval. |
| Ask | Make a neutral, policy-safe request | Ask every completed-job customer, not only obviously happy customers. No discounts, gift cards, or pressure. |
| Route | Send the customer to the correct review link | Use Google review link or QR code for most customers. Use Facebook/Nextdoor only if that is where the lead originated and Google review volume is already healthy. |
| Respond | Publicly answer every review | Positive reviews get specific gratitude. Negative or mixed reviews get calm acknowledgment, correction path, and offline resolution. |
| Repurpose | Convert reviews into proof assets | Turn strong reviews into website proof blocks, Meta ad snippets, GBP posts, estimate packet proof, and community group screenshots where allowed. |

**Post-job review workflow:**

1. **During final walkthrough:** Ask, "Is there anything you want us to touch up before we call this complete?" Do not ask for the review until the customer has had a chance to point out issues.
2. **After customer approval:** Say, "Reviews help local homeowners know who they can trust. If you feel we earned it, would you be willing to leave an honest Google review about your experience?"
3. **Within 10 minutes:** Text the direct Google review link. Google allows businesses to create and share a review request link or QR code to make it easier for customers to leave reviews ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)).
4. **After 24 hours if no review:** Send one gentle reminder. Do not ask for specific wording, star rating, or revision.
5. **After review posts:** Reply within 24 hours and tag the proof asset in the CRM by service type, city, and theme.
6. **If negative or mixed:** Reply publicly with accountability, then move resolution offline. Google says businesses can flag reviews only if they violate policy and should not report a review just because they disagree with it ([Google Business Profile review reporting guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)).

**Review request scripts:**

| Channel | Script |
|---|---|
| In person | "I’m glad we got everything wrapped up. Reviews are a huge help for a local company like ours. If you feel we earned it, would you be open to leaving an honest Google review? I’ll text you the link so it’s easy." |
| SMS, same day | "Hi [Name], this is [Owner/Estimator] with Valley Painting Pros. Thank you again for trusting us with your [cabinet/interior/exterior] project. If you feel we earned it, an honest Google review would mean a lot and helps other East Valley homeowners know who they can trust: [review link]" |
| SMS reminder | "Hi [Name], quick reminder from Valley Painting Pros. If you have a minute, your honest review would really help our local business: [review link]. Either way, thank you again for having us out." |
| Email | "Subject: Thank you for trusting Valley Painting Pros\n\nHi [Name], thank you again for trusting us with your [project type]. Reviews help local homeowners choose licensed, insured contractors with confidence. If you feel we earned it, you can leave an honest Google review here: [review link]." |

**Review response templates:**

| Review type | Response pattern |
|---|---|
| Positive cabinet review | "Thank you, [Name]. We appreciate you trusting Valley Painting Pros with your cabinet refinishing project in [City]. We’re glad the new finish gave the kitchen the updated look you wanted without replacing the cabinets." |
| Positive exterior review | "Thank you, [Name]. We appreciate the opportunity to paint your home in [City] and are glad the prep, cleanup, and final result met expectations." |
| Mixed review | "Thank you for the honest feedback, [Name]. We’re glad [positive point] and we’re sorry [issue] did not meet expectations. We’ll reach out directly to make this right." |
| Policy-violating review | "Do not argue publicly. Screenshot, document, flag through GBP if it violates policy, and keep the public response brief and professional." |

**Worked example: cabinet refinishing review flywheel**

A homeowner in Chandler hires VPP for a cabinet refinishing job. During the final walkthrough, the estimator asks if any doors need adjustment, handles two small touch-ups, and confirms satisfaction. The same day, the homeowner receives the Google review link by text. When the review mentions "clean prep," "licensed and insured," and "better than replacing cabinets," VPP replies publicly, adds the quote to the cabinet refinishing landing page, crops the before/after photos into a Meta carousel, and publishes a GBP post titled "Chandler cabinet refinishing project."

**When to use this vs. alternatives:**

| Use review flywheel when... | Use testimonial/case study framework when... | Use referral framework when... |
|---|---|---|
| The goal is to increase local trust, GBP prominence, and conversion proof after every job. | A customer has a detailed story, strong before/after photos, and permission for more specific use. | A customer knows neighbors or family likely to need painting and can make a direct introduction. |

**Advisor rule:** Every job should be considered incomplete until the review ask has happened, the customer has received the link, the public response is posted, and the proof asset is tagged.

### 10.3 Valley Painting Pros Brand Identity System

**Framework name:** Trustworthy Desert Craftsman Identity

**Creator / origin:** Applied brand identity system for Valley Painting Pros, based on the business strategy in this document: Arizona-local, licensed, insured, cabinet-refinishing-forward, professional but not corporate.

**Brand idea:** Valley Painting Pros should look like a clean, licensed Arizona contractor that homeowners can trust inside their kitchen, not like a discount painter, franchise chain, or generic handyman.

**Positioning sentence:**

> Valley Painting Pros is the licensed Arizona painting contractor for homeowners who want clean prep, durable finishes, and a contractor they can actually verify.

**Visual identity principles:**

| Principle | Rule | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed trust first | ROC, insured, bonded, review rating, and real project photos should appear more often than decorative graphics. | Local homeowners worry about contractor reliability more than clever branding. |
| Desert professional, not rustic | Use warm neutrals, muted teal, charcoal, and copper. Avoid distressed wood textures, cartoon paint splashes, or overly bright primary colors. | Signals Arizona locality without looking cheap or dated. |
| Cabinet refinishing proof | Lead with smooth cabinet finishes, prep shots, and before/after transformations. | Cabinet refinishing is the wedge service and has stronger visual contrast than many wall-painting jobs. |
| Human accountability | Use owner/crew photos, uniform shots, and branded vehicle shots. | Reduces contractor-anxiety and supports the local/family/craftsman archetype. |

**Recommended VPP color palette:**

| Role | Color name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary dark | Desert Charcoal | `#24211C` | Headlines, header text, footer background, premium contrast |
| Primary brand | ROC Teal | `#0E6F73` | CTAs, badges, links, trust accents |
| Background | Warm Stucco | `#F7F2E8` | Page background, cards, estimate forms |
| Surface | Cabinet Cream | `#FFFDF7` | Form fields, photo cards, proof sections |
| Accent | Copper Trim | `#B66A3C` | Small highlights, "licensed/bonded" accent, photo captions |
| Neutral | Primer Gray | `#D8D3C8` | Dividers, borders, secondary backgrounds |
| Text muted | Dust Gray | `#6F6A60` | Captions, helper text, secondary body |
| Success | Fresh Finish Green | `#3E7A4A` | Confirmation states, "estimate requested," "job complete" |

**Accessibility rule:** Use ROC Teal only on light backgrounds or as a filled CTA with white text after contrast testing. Use Copper Trim sparingly for emphasis, not body text.

**Recommended font pairing:**

| Use case | Font | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Website headings | Satoshi, General Sans, or Avenir-style geometric sans | Modern, clean, professional without looking like a tech company. |
| Website body | Source Sans 3, Inter fallback, or system sans fallback | Highly readable on mobile for service pages and estimate forms. |
| Estimate PDFs / proposals | Arial or Calibri | Reliable rendering across customer devices. |
| Yard signs / truck lettering | Bold geometric sans in all or title case | Fast recognition from street distance. |

**Logo usage rules:**

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| Clear space | Keep at least the height of the "V" or icon mark around the logo on all sides. |
| Minimum size | Website header logo should remain readable at 140-180px wide on desktop and 110-140px wide on mobile. |
| Background | Use full-color logo on Warm Stucco or Cabinet Cream. Use one-color white logo on Desert Charcoal or dark photo overlays. |
| Do not | Do not stretch, add drop shadows, place over busy before/after images without overlay, recolor randomly, or add "licensed/insured" inside the logo mark. |
| Lockup | Keep ROC license, insured, bonded, and review badges as a trust bar near the logo or CTA, not part of the logo itself. |

**Applied layout rules:**

| Asset | VPP identity application |
|---|---|
| Website hero | Warm Stucco background, Desert Charcoal headline, ROC Teal CTA, real cabinet before/after image, trust bar below CTA. |
| Meta static ad | Large before/after photo, small ROC Teal badge "Licensed, Bonded & Insured," short headline on Cabinet Cream panel, CTA in ROC Teal. |
| Door hanger | Top: "Cabinet Refinishing in Your Neighborhood." Middle: before/after. Bottom: QR code, phone, ROC trust bar. Use Copper Trim only for "local project nearby." |
| Estimate packet | Clean white/Cabinet Cream layout, dark headings, proof sidebar with reviews, ROC, insurance, warranty. |

**Worked example: cabinet refinishing ad look**

Use a split before/after image of oak cabinets becoming warm white cabinets. Place a small top-left badge: "ROC Licensed · Bonded · Insured." Put the headline in a Cabinet Cream block: "Refresh Your Kitchen Without Replacing Your Cabinets." CTA button: "Get a Free Cabinet Estimate." Use ROC Teal for the button, Desert Charcoal for text, and no more than one Copper Trim accent line.

**When to use this vs. alternatives:**

| Use VPP identity system when... | Use general visual design principles when... | Use campaign-specific art direction when... |
|---|---|---|
| Creating anything public-facing for Valley Painting Pros. | Auditing layout, hierarchy, accessibility, or photo quality independent of brand. | A temporary campaign needs a seasonal look, such as monsoon exterior prep or holiday cabinet refresh. |

**Advisor rule:** If a new asset does not look licensed, local, clean, and cabinet-capable within three seconds, revise it before publishing.

### 10.4 Lead Response and Intake SLA Framework

**Framework name:** 0-5-15-24 Speed-to-Estimate System

**Creator / origin:** Lead response management research plus contractor intake operations. InsideSales reports that conversion rates are 8x greater in the first five minutes and that only 0.1% of inbound leads are actually engaged in under five minutes in its 2021 lead response research ([InsideSales](https://www.insidesales.com/response-time-matters/)). Lead response research summarized by Revenue.io states that leads contacted within five minutes are 100x more likely to be reached than leads contacted after 30 minutes, and 21x more likely to qualify ([Revenue.io](https://www.revenue.io/inside-sales-glossary/what-is-lead-response-time)).

**Core SLA:**

| Time window | Required action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 0 minutes | Lead enters CRM or lead sheet with source, service, city, phone, and timestamp. Auto-reply fires instantly. | Automation |
| Under 5 minutes | First human call attempt for all hot leads: Meta lead form, website estimate request, GBP call/message, Google LSA, Nextdoor paid lead. | Owner or intake lead |
| Under 15 minutes | If no answer, send SMS and second call attempt. | Owner or intake lead |
| Same day | Third call attempt, voicemail, email, and schedule-link follow-up. | Intake |
| 24 hours | Final short follow-up and disqualify/nurture if no response. | Intake |
| 7 days | Recycle into nurture list if not booked. | CRM |

**Lead source priority:**

| Priority | Lead source | Response target | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phone calls from GBP, Google LSA, website click-to-call | Answer live whenever possible | Caller is high intent and comparing vendors now. |
| 2 | Website estimate form | Call under 5 minutes | High intent, owned traffic, likely ready to schedule. |
| 3 | Meta Instant Form | Call under 5 minutes | Lower friction means lower commitment; speed filters serious leads. |
| 4 | Community FB group referral/comment | Message under 15 minutes | Trust is high but urgency varies. |
| 5 | Door hanger QR/form | Call under 15 minutes | Local relevance is high; intent depends on offer. |
| 6 | Old nurture/review/referral lead | Same day | Warm but not necessarily active. |

**Intake script structure:**

| Step | Script |
|---|---|
| Open | "Hi [Name], this is [Owner/Estimator] with Valley Painting Pros. You requested a [cabinet/interior/exterior] painting estimate a few minutes ago. Is now still a good time?" |
| Confirm need | "What are you hoping to update, and what made you start looking now?" |
| Qualify scope | "Is this cabinets, interior walls, exterior, or a mix? About how many rooms / cabinet doors / square feet?" |
| Location | "What city or cross streets are you near?" |
| Timing | "Are you looking to get this done soon, or are you pricing options for later?" |
| Trust | "We’re ROC licensed, bonded, and insured in Arizona, and I can include our ROC number on the written estimate." |
| Close for next step | "The best next step is a free on-site estimate so we can give you a fixed written scope. I have [two times]. Which works better?" |

**Auto-reply templates:**

| Source | Auto-reply |
|---|---|
| Website form | "Thanks for requesting an estimate from Valley Painting Pros. We received your request and will call shortly. If you want faster help, call/text [phone]. ROC licensed, bonded, and insured." |
| Meta Instant Form | "Thanks, [Name]. Valley Painting Pros received your cabinet refinishing request. We’ll call shortly to learn about your project and schedule a free estimate. You can also call/text [phone]." |
| After-hours | "Thanks for reaching out to Valley Painting Pros. We received your request after hours and will follow up first thing tomorrow. If this is urgent, text [phone] with 'estimate' and your project type." |

**Disposition statuses:**

| Status | Definition | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| New | Lead received, no contact attempt yet | Call immediately |
| Attempted | Call placed, no answer | SMS + second attempt |
| Contacted | Two-way conversation happened | Qualify and schedule |
| Estimate scheduled | On-site or virtual estimate booked | Send confirmation |
| Estimate completed | Estimate delivered | Follow proposal follow-up cadence |
| Won | Deposit or verbal approval received | Schedule production |
| Lost | Chose competitor, price issue, bad fit | Capture reason |
| Nurture | Not ready yet | Follow up monthly or seasonally |
| Bad fit | Outside area, wrong service, spam | Exclude from paid optimization if possible |

**KPI dashboard:**

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Median time to first response | Under 5 minutes during business hours |
| SLA compliance | 90%+ of hot leads called within 5 minutes |
| Contact rate by source | Track weekly |
| Estimate booking rate by source | Track weekly |
| Estimate show rate | Track weekly |
| Close rate by source | Track monthly |
| Lost reason distribution | Review monthly |

**Worked example: Meta cabinet refinishing lead**

A homeowner submits a Meta Instant Form after seeing a cabinet before/after ad. The auto-reply fires instantly. Within three minutes, VPP calls and says, "You requested a cabinet refinishing estimate a few minutes ago. Is now still a good time?" If the homeowner does not answer, VPP sends a text with the project-specific callback prompt and calls again 10 minutes later. Once reached, intake confirms number of doors/drawers, city, current cabinet finish, desired color, timing, and schedules a free on-site estimate. The CRM status moves from New to Estimate Scheduled, and the lead source remains "Meta - Cabinet Refinishing - [Ad Name]."

**When to use this vs. alternatives:**

| Use SLA framework when... | Use sales script framework when... | Use nurture framework when... |
|---|---|---|
| A new inbound lead arrives or the question concerns lead handling speed, routing, status, or booking. | The homeowner is already on the phone and needs persuasion, objection handling, or scope explanation. | The lead is not ready, missed, old, seasonal, or price-shopping for later. |

**Advisor rule:** Paid ads should not be scaled until the intake SLA is working. A slow callback turns Meta, GBP, and Google leads into wasted spend.
